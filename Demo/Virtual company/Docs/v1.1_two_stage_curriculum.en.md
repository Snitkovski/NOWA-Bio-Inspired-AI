# NOWA Procurement Sandbox v1.1: two-stage curriculum

Russian version: [v1.1_two_stage_curriculum.ru.md](v1.1_two_stage_curriculum.ru.md)

## Abstract

This note sets out the sandbox's two-stage curriculum and collects outcomes already measured. In version 1.0, graduation required six criteria in one quarter and, in the recorded run of thirty quarters, occurred twice. Re-scoring that same run on the four criteria that do not depend on a stable `supplier_id` yields graduation in seven of thirty quarters. Stage 1a leaves suppliers rotating and gates graduation on cost efficiency, stockout avoidance, budget discipline, and reaction to risk. Stage 1b carries a supplier's attributes across quarters, so that an identifier denotes one counterparty, and gates graduation on decision consistency and diversification. The four Stage 1a criteria are checked for non-regression against 0.9 of the baseline. Days, budget, and open orders reset with the new world; when a roster is carried, only the default flag is cleared. Stage 1b starts after Stage 1a graduation.

Five independent Stage 1a runs of thirty quarters, seeds 1–5, continued past the first pass, graduated seeds 1, 4, and 5. The first pass fell in quarters 6, 3, and 9; the numbers of passing quarters are 5, 4, and 3. Three of five meet the minimum. Graduation quarters 3, 6, and 9 deviate from their mean by no more than 50%, that is, exactly on the tolerance bound. The baseline was taken from seed 1, the median of the three graduated runs: cost efficiency 0.752, stockout avoidance 0.949, budget discipline 1.000, and reaction to risk 0.789. Seed 2 never recovered from early stockouts, and its quarters ran about three times more slowly than the others because a permanently under-stocked world generates more events per day.

Twenty Stage 1b quarters resumed from the seed-1 checkpoint raised mean diversification from 0.323 on Stage 1a to 0.513; eight of twenty quarters passed the two gating criteria. Under the single-`all_passed` rule, training would have stopped at quarter 31. Re-scoring under the v1.1.1 streak rule yields a qualifying streak of 0 for the whole trial: stockout avoidance is below the floor in all twenty quarters, cost efficiency in eight, and reaction to risk in five. Budget discipline equals 1.000 in every quarter of the trial, and the question of carrying budget is closed with the default: budget is not carried between Stage 1b quarters. Re-scoring the same quarters after patch v1.2 reduces stockout guard failures from 9/20 to 0/20; cost efficiency still fails in every one of the twenty quarters and remains the blocking criterion. By the requirement that graduation be held, Stage 1b is not ready for a further transition. Loading a raw Stage 1a list that contains no graduated quarter is now refused as a floor.

## Why

In v1.0, graduation required all six fitness criteria to pass in the same
quarter. In the recorded 30-quarter run, this happened in only 2 of 30
quarters.

The two relationship criteria, `decision_consistency` and
`supplier_diversification`, count choices by `supplier_id`. That identity is
not stable in the Stage 1a world: each quarter creates suppliers with fresh
reliability, price, and lead-time attributes while reusing the same ID strings.
What the agent learned about `supplier_002` in one quarter could therefore
refer to a different supplier in the next.

Recomputing that recorded run with only the four identity-independent criteria
as gates yields graduation in 7 of 30 quarters. The curriculum separates
general procurement competence from relationship-building competence so each is
measured in a world where it can exist.

## The two stages

Stage 1a is the "trade school": suppliers are regenerated each quarter. It
gates on the criteria that can be assessed without a stable supplier identity.

Stage 1b is the "internship": suppliers persist across quarters, so their
attributes and order history follow the same `supplier_id` from one quarter to
the next. It gates on the relationship criteria. The four Stage 1a criteria
remain measured and are checked for non-regression, so relationship scores
cannot improve by making poor purchases.

Stage 1b runs only after Stage 1a graduation. The mixed regime, in which
persistent and disposable worlds alternate within one run, is deferred to v1.2
until both stages are confirmed separately.

| Criterion | Gating stage |
|---|---|
| `cost_efficiency` | Stage 1a |
| `stockout_avoidance` | Stage 1a |
| `budget_discipline` | Stage 1a |
| `reaction_to_risk_events` | Stage 1a |
| `decision_consistency` | Stage 1b |
| `supplier_diversification` | Stage 1b |

`decision_consistency` measures whether the agent chooses the same
`supplier_id` for a product in similar situations. `supplier_diversification`
measures how orders are distributed between `supplier_id` values. Both require
the ID to refer to the same supplier across quarters.

## Reading a graduation report

Every report contains all six criteria, regardless of stage. Each criterion has
its value, threshold, `passed` result, and a `gating` boolean.

`gating: true` means the criterion contributes to `all_passed` for the current
stage. Criteria with `gating: false` are still reported for diagnosis. In Stage
1a, the four general-procurement criteria gate graduation; in Stage 1b, the two
relationship criteria gate it.

## The non-regression guard

`check_non_regression` compares every Stage 1a criterion with 90% of its
`stage_1a_baseline` value (`NON_REGRESSION_FLOOR = 0.9`). A Stage 1b run must
therefore preserve cost efficiency, stockout avoidance, budget discipline, and
risk response while developing supplier relationships.

The baseline is the mean of the four Stage 1a criteria over the last five
successful Stage 1a quarters. The baseline helper records the selected quarter
indices and whether they were successful; when a run has no successful
quarters, it can instead derive a marked exploratory baseline from its trailing
quarters. The sweep-report command writes a baseline only when it has a
graduated run to select.

## Commands

Run these commands from `Demo/Virtual company/`.

Run a Stage 1a sweep across five seeds. `--no-stop-on-graduation` matters here:
the baseline averages the last five *successful* quarters, so the run has to
continue past its first pass instead of halting there.

```bash
for SEED in 1 2 3 4 5; do
  python src/nowa_sandbox/run_sandbox.py \
    --config configs/default_world.yaml \
    --stage 1a \
    --quarters 30 \
    --seed $SEED \
    --no-stop-on-graduation \
    --output results/stage1a_seed${SEED}.json \
    --checkpoint checkpoints/stage1a_seed${SEED}.ckpt
done
```

Summarize the result files and evaluate the Stage 1a readiness checks:

```bash
python src/nowa_sandbox/stage1a_report.py results/stage1a_seed*.json
```

Write the baseline from the median graduated run:

```bash
python src/nowa_sandbox/stage1a_report.py results/stage1a_seed*.json \
  --write-baseline results/stage_1a_baseline.json
```

`training.stage` in `configs/default_world.yaml` selects the default stage, and
`--stage` overrides it.

## Stage 1b execution

Stage 1b keeps the same day-by-day loop and still rebuilds the world every
quarter, so days, budget and pending orders reset exactly as in Stage 1a
(section 26.6.1 keeps the budget per-quarter). What changes is that supplier
attributes are exported at the end of a quarter and re-adopted by the next
one, so `supplier_002` is the same counterparty throughout the run, and its
`reputation` and `total_orders` accumulate where the agent can observe them.

Only `is_defaulted` is reset when a roster is carried over. A default is a bad
quarter rather than a life sentence, and the agent needs the opportunity to
watch a previously risky supplier earn trust back.

`--baseline-from` is required for `--stage 1b`; the run is refused without it,
because an unguarded Stage 1b could raise its relationship scores by buying
badly. It accepts either a baseline file or a raw Stage 1a result JSON.

```bash
python src/nowa_sandbox/run_sandbox.py \
  --stage 1b \
  --baseline-from results/stage_1a_baseline.json \
  --quarters 30 \
  --no-stop-on-graduation \
  --output results/stage1b_run.json \
  --checkpoint checkpoints/stage1b_run.ckpt
```

To continue from an agent that has already been through Stage 1a, add
`--resume-from` pointing at that Stage 1a checkpoint. The carried supplier
roster is stored in the checkpoint alongside the agent, so resuming a Stage 1b
run keeps its counterparties instead of silently reverting to Stage 1a
behaviour.

Each quarter of a Stage 1b run records a `non_regression` block next to its
`graduation` block in the output JSON, and the printed summary appends
`REGRESSED: <criteria>` to any quarter that fell through the floor.

Section 26.7 asks Stage 1b graduation to be *held* rather than hit once, since
the original diagnosis was precisely a pair of isolated lucky quarters.
Patch v1.1.1 makes that binding on `result.graduated` itself: a Stage 1b
quarter only counts toward graduation when it both passes its gating criteria
and clears the non-regression guard, and graduation fires only after
`GRADUATION_MIN_STREAK` (3) consecutive qualifying quarters. The streak is
recomputed from `result.quarters` on every call, so a checkpoint resume keeps
credit for quarters already run. Stage 1a is unchanged — one `all_passed` is
still enough.

The printed summary shows `qualifying_streak=N` after every quarter, and
`regressed_quarters()` lists passing quarters that were bought at the cost of
Stage 1a competence.

## Measured results

### Stage 1a readiness (section 26.7, first gate)

Five independent 30-quarter runs, seeds 1 to 5, `--no-stop-on-graduation`.
Criteria columns are the baseline means over the trailing successful quarters.

| run | graduated | first pass | passing quarters | cost_eff | stockout_avoid | budget_disc | risk_react |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| seed 1 | yes | 6 | 5 | 0.752 | 0.949 | 1.000 | 0.789 |
| seed 2 | no | – | 0 | 0.562 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 0.065 |
| seed 3 | no | – | 0 | 0.386 | 0.220 | 1.000 | 0.664 |
| seed 4 | yes | 3 | 4 | 0.841 | 0.973 | 1.000 | 0.762 |
| seed 5 | yes | 9 | 3 | 0.682 | 0.926 | 1.000 | 0.856 |

Three of five graduated, meeting the minimum. Graduation quarters were 3, 6 and
9, a maximum deviation of 50% from their mean, which sits exactly on the
tolerance limit rather than comfortably inside it. The baseline was taken from
seed 1, the median of the three graduated runs.

Seed 2 is worth noting: it never recovered from early stockouts, and its
quarters ran roughly three times slower than the others because a permanently
under-stocked world generates far more events per day.

### Stage 1b trial (section 27.4)

Twenty Stage 1b quarters resumed from the seed 1 Stage 1a checkpoint.

Persistent suppliers do what they were introduced to do:
`supplier_diversification` rose from a Stage 1a mean of 0.323 to 0.513, and
eight of twenty quarters passed the two gating criteria.

Under v1.1 the trainer still set `result.graduated` from a single
`all_passed`, so the same run would have stopped at quarter 31 with
`stop_on_graduation=True` despite every Stage 1b quarter having regressed
`stockout_avoidance`. Patch v1.1.1 closes that gap: recomputing the recorded
20 Stage 1b quarters under the new rule yields a qualifying streak of 0 in
every quarter, so `result.graduated` stays `False` for the whole trial.

Stage 1b is nonetheless **not ready** by section 26.7:

- The longest run of consecutive *gating* passes was 2, short of the required
  3, and under v1.1.1 the qualifying streak never leaves 0.
- `stockout_avoidance` fell below its non-regression floor in all 20 quarters,
  `cost_efficiency` in 8, `reaction_to_risk_events` in 5.
- No quarter was simultaneously passing and clean under the guard.

This is the guard working as designed rather than a defect: the agent improved
its relationship metrics partly by concentrating orders and letting stock
coverage lapse, which is precisely the trade section 26.5 exists to forbid.
Closing this gap is an agent-behaviour calibration problem, not a mechanism
problem, and belongs to the next revision.

Section 26.6.1 can be closed with its recommended default: budget is not
carried between Stage 1b quarters. `budget_discipline` held at 1.000 in every
quarter of the trial, so the per-quarter reset caused no observable trouble.

`--baseline-from` on a raw Stage 1a results list now refuses files with no
graduated quarters (symmetric with `stage1a_report.py --write-baseline`), so a
failed Stage 1a sweep cannot silently become a Stage 1b floor of near-zero.

### Patch v1.2 — honest baseline

The v1.1.1 streak logic was correct, but the Stage 1a floor it guarded against
was not: mean-of-best stockout (~0.949) treated normal Stage 1a noise as
regression. v1.2 switches to median-of-all + IQR-aware floors. Re-scoring the
same Stage 1b seed1 quarters drops stockout guard failures from 9/20 to 0/20;
`cost_efficiency` still fails all 20 and remains the Stage 1b blocker. Details
and Direction B diagnostics: `results/RESULTS_v12.md`.
